# Critique findings and what draft 1.1 did with each

Two critics reviewed draft 1 on 23 Oct 2026. One checked it against the Build Scope section by section. The other checked platform limits, security and recovery from a failed run. Every finding is listed here with its disposition. "Adopted" means draft 1.1 carries the fix the critic proposed, or one that closes the same gap.

## Completeness against the scope

The plan covers most of the scope. All 15 scope entities, the ten reason codes, the six forms, the nine user-facing screens and the pilot-metrics table each map to a table, a route or a workstream, and nothing in it needs gallery-os or engine code. It fails three hard checks. (1) The first-session checklist cannot run from cold. Step 2 copies files from this session's /tmp scratchpad, and the plan itself is not committed to the repo. Steps 2 to 4 also never change the proof's proxy (matcher /dashboard, redirect to /) or its ping route (raw SYNC_TOKEN), yet step 4 expects /app to redirect to /sign-in and the hashed-token ping to pass. (2) Deletion leaves personal data behind, which breaks the scope's rule that deletion removes both copies. It survives in sync_event payloads (referral names, notes, survey answers, approved tailored copy and cover letters), in access_log, in the weekly manual Neon snapshot the plan itself adds, and in Resend's 30-day retention. (3) From day 1 the plan puts pathtoimpact.us on Vercel Production with the $175 Capital Clarity page at the root until week 6. That is the commercial use the plan's own decision 2 says threatens Hobby, while decision 2 says hosting blocks nothing before week 3. Against the scope's 13 decisions, only 10, 11 and 13 are presented as decisions. Decisions 1 to 5 (the profile rulings) and 8 (SerpApi) are dropped. For SerpApi, section 1 says it appears in section 8, and it does not. Decisions 6 (Kem as user zero), 7 (splitting the live engine) and 9 (the staffing statement) are assumed. Decision 12 ("backend") is decided in assumption 1. The Kem-hours reconciliation does not add up. It leaves out phase 0's 12 to 16 hours and the time to review every pull request. It contradicts WS11's 7 Kem-days. Its own M4 cell says "about 8" and "9 to 15" hours. Further gaps: the four-weekly process survey is missing, the four-week profile review has no coverage data from the engine, the overlay field list is never written down, and nothing builds the landing page. Logic defects: late pushes break the week key and the digest, the 3-a-day per-address mail cap drops build and operator notices, preview auth breaks on per-pull-request hosts, and decision cross-references point at the wrong decisions.

## Feasibility and security

The plan is thorough and mostly grounded, but it does not survive as written. Six high-severity problems.

1. The first-session checklist cannot run from a fresh session. It copies files from this session's scratchpad and reads a plan that is not in the repo.
2. Sessions cannot reach Neon at all today. I tested it: the session proxy refuses CONNECT with a 403 for neon.tech, console.neon.tech and endpoint-shaped *.neon.tech hosts. That leaves every "session runs migrations or database tests" step without a path, and the claim that "only the Neon HTTP path is known to work" is false.
3. Deletion leaves personal data behind. sync_event payloads (decision text, referral names, draft cover letters) and access_log rows are kept on purpose with no foreign key, so a deleted user's data survives.
4. Seven of the scope's 13 decisions are dropped: Eval Ops, B1, V1, V2, L1, the live engine and SerpApi. Section 1 says they appear in section 8 as needs; they do not. Scope decisions 9 and 12 are settled without being flagged.
5. The digest claim cannot recover from a send that dies partway, so the remaining users silently get nothing that week.
6. A public health check that queries the database would keep Neon Free awake around the clock. That is about 180 CU-hours a month against the 100 allowed.

Medium-severity issues:
- Recovery after a run that dies halfway has gaps: a build can sit at ready with no PDF, a bad PDF can never be replaced, proposals from a dropped pick stay proposed, the event cursor can skip events committed out of order, a retried deletion ack gets a 404, and a late push is blocked by the skip-week digest.
- Preview auth breaks with a fixed BETTER_AUTH_URL.
- The interim layout deploys the paid Capital Clarity page on the new Hobby project.
- Impersonation can forge consent and "I applied" rows.
- Email links that act on a plain click will fire when mail scanners prefetch them.
- There is no re-consent path when the consent text changes.
- The landing, privacy and terms pages have no workstream.
- The TS scripts have no runner (verified).
- Kem's hours do not add up: the reconciliation uses the scope's 20 to 25 engine hours instead of the plan's own 7 Kem-days, and leaves out phase 0 work and PR review.
- Decision cross-references are misnumbered.

What holds up:
- The sync contract can be built with Python's standard library alone: JSON, plus raw-byte PUTs through urllib. No multipart and no base64 are needed.
- The 4,194,304-byte cap respects Vercel's 4.5 MB limit.
- The crons respect Hobby's once-a-day minimum.
- No step in this repo needs gallery-os or the engine code.
